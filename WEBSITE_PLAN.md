# Dog Training Website Plan

## Confirmed direction — 6 October 2026

- **Business:** JR Dog Training
- **Base:** Bonnells Bay, NSW
- **Service area:** Morisset and surrounding areas
- **Primary focus:** One-to-one dog training and practical behaviour support
- **Service:** One-to-one dog training delivered as a house visit
- **Session:** One hour, from $112
- **Travel:** Add $15 when travel time from Bonnells Bay exceeds 30 minutes
- **Service format:** House visits only; no services at the trainer's home
- **Booking route:** The existing Mad Paws profile
- **Brand assets:** No logo or social profiles yet; use a clean typographic wordmark for version one
- **Experience:** Ten years of practical dog-training experience
- **Positioning boundary:** Self-taught trainer, not a certified dog behaviourist or veterinary behaviourist; refer dogs outside current experience or safe working scope to an appropriate specialist
- **Current proof:** First two clients are underway; do not publish reviews or outcome claims until genuine feedback and permission are available

Source material: [current Mad Paws profile](https://www.madpaws.com.au/petsitter/bonnells-bay-nsw/james-r-reactive-dog-dog-behaviour-training-free-first-consultation), checked 6 October 2026.

## 1. The job of the first website

The first version has one primary job: turn a local dog owner who has heard about the business into a confident enquiry.

It should quickly answer:

1. Can this trainer help with my dog and my problem?
2. Do I trust their approach?
3. Do they work in my area?
4. What happens next, and how do I get started?

Primary conversion: **book a one-hour house visit through Mad Paws**.

Secondary conversion: **view the Mad Paws profile for current availability, pricing, and booking protection**.

## 2. Recommended version-one scope

Build a polished, mobile-first single-page site, plus a simple enquiry-success page. A focused one-page site is enough for the current stage and will be easier to improve using feedback from the first clients.

### Page structure

1. **Hero**
   - Clear outcome-led headline
   - One sentence on who the service is for and the service area
   - Primary call to action
   - Warm, real image of the trainer working with a dog

2. **Problems we can work on**
   - Only list services genuinely offered
   - Use the language owners use, not training jargon
   - Examples might include puppy foundations, pulling on lead, recall, settling, and everyday manners—but the final list needs to reflect the actual offer

3. **How the training works**
   - A simple three-step process: enquire, meet and assess, practise with support
   - Explain the training philosophy in plain language
   - Set expectations without making guaranteed-outcome claims

4. **Ways to work together**
   - Start with no more than two or three offers
   - For each: who it is for, what is included, location/format, duration, and price or “from” price if pricing is public

5. **Why trust this trainer**
   - Short personal story
   - Relevant qualifications, experience, insurance, memberships, or approach—only where accurate
   - Real photography rather than generic stock imagery wherever possible

6. **Early proof**
   - Add feedback from the first two clients once there is permission and a genuine result to report
   - A short, specific quote is more useful than a vague endorsement
   - Do not hold up launch waiting for testimonials; this section can initially explain the client-centred approach

7. **Frequently asked questions**
   - Service area
   - Where sessions happen
   - What to bring
   - Whether the family can attend
   - Vaccination/age expectations, if relevant
   - Cancellation and rescheduling basics
   - What happens after an enquiry

8. **Final enquiry section**
   - Repeat the primary action
   - Ask only for information needed to respond: owner name, contact details, suburb, dog name/age, main challenge, and preferred next step
   - State the expected response time only if it can be met consistently

9. **Footer**
   - Business name and contact details
   - Service area
   - Social links, if actively maintained
   - Privacy notice and business details where applicable

## 3. Visitor journey

```text
Referral / search / social post
          ↓
Recognises their problem in the opening screen
          ↓
Understands the approach and available service
          ↓
Sees genuine reasons to trust the trainer
          ↓
Sends a low-friction enquiry
          ↓
Receives a clear acknowledgement and personal follow-up
```

Every major section should move the visitor through this sequence. Avoid duplicate sales pitches, filler sections, and features that do not help someone decide or enquire.

## 4. Content and assets to collect

### Essential before the build is final

- Business name and any existing logo or colours
- Trainer name, short biography, and a friendly headshot
- Service area and travel limits
- The exact services currently offered
- Prices, or a decision to discuss price after enquiry
- Preferred enquiry channel: form, phone, SMS, email, or another channel
- Contact details and availability
- Training philosophy and methods in the trainer's own words
- Any relevant qualifications, insurance, memberships, or experience that can be stated publicly
- Cancellation, rescheduling, and basic service policies

### Capture during the first two client engagements

- A short note on the owner's starting problem, desired outcome, and progress
- Natural photos or short video, with explicit permission for website use
- The phrases clients use to describe their problem and why they chose the service
- A testimonial request after value has been delivered—not before
- Written permission specifying whether first name, dog name, suburb, photo, and quote may be published

## 5. Visual direction

Aim for **calm, capable, local, and encouraging** rather than cute or overly clinical.

- Mobile-first layout with large readable type and obvious tap targets
- Warm natural photography, ideally from real sessions
- A restrained palette inspired by outdoor training: warm neutral, deep green or blue, and one clear action colour
- Plenty of breathing room and short sections
- Subtle movement only when it improves orientation or feedback
- Accessible colour contrast, keyboard navigation, labelled fields, and meaningful image descriptions

The site should feel like the trainer: approachable enough to contact, professional enough to trust with a difficult problem.

## 6. Build approach

Recommended implementation for version one:

- A lightweight static website with no account system
- Responsive HTML/CSS/JavaScript, with minimal dependencies
- A private preview first, then publication once content and contact routing have been tested
- Basic search metadata, social-share image, favicon, and local-business wording
- Simple privacy-conscious traffic measurement only if it will actually be reviewed
- Spam protection on any public enquiry form

The first version does **not** need online payments, customer accounts, a blog, a course library, automated scheduling, or a complex content-management system. These can be added when repeated demand makes the benefit clear.

## 7. Delivery sequence

### Phase 1 — Define the offer

- Confirm the target client, service area, offers, prices, and primary contact action
- Agree on the business name and initial visual direction
- Draft the core promise without guarantees or inflated claims

Output: approved one-page content outline.

### Phase 2 — Gather real material

- Take a small set of strong photos
- Write the trainer story and service descriptions
- Capture initial client language, feedback, and permissions
- Prepare policies and contact details

Output: a complete content and asset folder.

### Phase 3 — Build the private first version

- Create the responsive page and success state
- Add enquiry routing
- Add accessibility, search, privacy, and performance basics
- Publish privately for review

Output: working private preview on phone and desktop.

### Phase 4 — Test and launch

- Test every link, form, phone number, and email route
- Test on a real phone and at common screen sizes
- Ask one or two people unfamiliar with the business to find the offer, location, and next step
- Correct confusing copy and launch publicly

Output: live version-one site.

### Phase 5 — Improve from evidence

- Review the quality of enquiries, not just visitor numbers
- Add genuine client stories and stronger photos
- Adjust the headline, services, FAQs, and call to action based on repeated questions
- Consider booking, deposits, dedicated service pages, or local search content only after the need appears

## 8. Launch checklist

- The offer, location, and next step are clear without scrolling on a phone
- All claims are accurate and all published client material has permission
- Contact submissions reach the correct place and receive an acknowledgement
- Phone, email, social, navigation, and policy links work
- The site works with keyboard navigation and readable contrast
- Images are compressed and the page loads quickly on mobile data
- Page title, description, sharing image, favicon, and basic local-business details are present
- There is a privacy notice appropriate to the data collected
- No placeholder copy, fake reviews, stock statistics, or unverified credentials remain

## 9. Measures for the first 90 days

Keep measurement small and useful:

- Number of qualified enquiries
- Where each enquirer heard about the business
- Enquiry-to-first-session conversion
- Most common problem or requested service
- Questions that were not answered by the site
- Contact attempts that failed or were abandoned

The first meaningful success threshold is not a traffic number. It is that suitable local owners understand the offer and can confidently take the next step.
